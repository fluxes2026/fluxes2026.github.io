Keywords: Ecosystem Respiration, Gross Primary Productivity, forest, wetlands, RuFlux, FLUXNET, MODIS

 

The carbon balance of an ecosystem is the difference between Gross Primary Productivity (GPP) and Ecosystem Respiration (Reco) as expressed by Net Ecosystem Exchange (NEE). While remote sensing retrievals of GPP have reached maturity, Reco estimation remains underexplored and ultimately cast bias on NEE. Reco consists of a number of diverse respiration components and their decoupling, parameterization and choice of appropriate predictors is challenging. Here we present an end-to-end multi-scale analysis of the mechanism of Reco implemented at the level of eddy covariance tower measurements and scaled up to country level by means of remote sensing. Study has been performed over Russian forests and wetlands. Carbon flux data were collected from 20 RuFlux sites over Russia and 46 FLUXNET sites over Europe and North America with similar climatic conditions. We utilized three regression models fit to daily tower Reco over the annual cycle: 1) linear model forced by daily productivity (GPP), 2) Arrhenius exponential model forced by daily average Air Temperature (TA), 3) general model, a linear combination of the above. Our analysis indicates that best performance is achieved by the general model, while the first two models serve as components accounting for respiration of the aboveground biomass and soil, respectively. With a limited accuracy components may function in a stand-alone mode due to cross-correlation of predictors. Experiments with the general model indicate that ecosystems exhibit distinct mechanisms of carbon fluxes generation, expressed in automatic “lean” on GPP or TA driven components over the course of the annual cycle. We also found that approximation of annual Reco profile is possible only with piece-wise function, where “break-up point” (TA=0oC) separate regimes with active photosynthesis from the rest condition. The retrieved Reco models’ parameters exhibit a natural temporal and spatial variability, caused by variations of ecosystem and atmospheric conditions. To implement into large scale algorithm, we aggregated set of models’ parameters by plant functional types (PFT) and regressed on the annual GPP, a predictor of fluxes’ spatio-temporal variability. Daily and annual GPP/Reco/NEE products were generated from MODIS remote sensing and MERRA2 reanalysis data. Reco exhibits lower amplitude and more inert spatial variability compared to that of GPP. At the annual scale Russian forests and wetlands serve as carbon sink. All three annual carbon fluxes have tendency to increase (by absolute value) from north to south with highest values observed at European part. Country-wide spatial statistics indicate that on the annual scale WET have GPP close to Reco and therefore are most open to change NEE sign, while DBF have GPP significantly prevail Reco to remain most stable as a carbon sink. Still, actual implementation of NEE sign change is subject to persistence of ecosystems to changes and external forcing. Future research on the components of soil respiration will help reduce Reco remaining uncertainties.
